Where 311 runs slow

Which parts of Memphis wait longer than the rest of the city for the same kind of 311 request, and whether those places cluster.

Requests opened 1 June 2026 to 31 August 2026Data through 24 Sep 202637,270 requests · 181 census tractsBuilt 28 Sep 2026

From June to August 2026, 311 requests in some parts of Memphis closed much faster or slower than the same kinds of request elsewhere. These places sit together in two large clusters.

  • Fast cluster, north: 30 tracts, mostly in ZIPs 38127 (Frayser) and 38128 (Raleigh). 43% fewer requests than expected ran past the citywide typical time for their type.
  • Slow cluster, southwest: 29 tracts, mostly in ZIPs 38116 (Whitehaven) and 38109. 31% more did.
  • The gap is mostly trash pickup. A bulk-trash request closed in a median of 1 business day in the fast cluster, 7 business days citywide and 15 business days in the slow cluster.
  • Part of the gap may be record-keeping. The two clusters close these requests with different codes in the City's system (Figure 3B), so faster closing may not mean faster pickup.
  • The fast cluster is stable; the slow one moves. A year earlier the fast cluster was in the same place, while the slow cluster was mostly in ZIPs 38114, 38111 and 38104 (Midtown).

The question

Does how long the City takes to close a 311 request depend on where it was filed, when each request is compared only with requests of the same type?

Slow request
Not closed within the citywide median time for its type, counted in City business days (D10). A request still open counts as slow once that time has passed.
Eligible request
Opened in the period and old enough to judge: its type's typical time has passed.
Expected
The number of slow requests a tract would have if each of its requests fared like the city average for its type.
Ratio
Slow ÷ expected. 1.0 is average; 1.3 means 30% more slow requests than expected.
Areas
2020 census tracts, clipped to the city limits. 205 overlap the city; 181 have at least 30 eligible requests and are analysed.
Period
Requests opened 1 June 2026 to 31 August 2026; data through 24 September 2026. 37,270 eligible requests of 61 types.

Comparing like with like matters because types differ a lot: a pothole typically closes in 1 business day, a weeds case in 26 business days. Without this, an area would look slow just because it reports more weeds cases.

Why this question

  • Chosen. It measures something residents experience, uses 37,270 requests with exact locations, and needs no population denominator.
  • Not chosen: profiles combining 311, permits and inspections. The sources cover different periods (3 months, 5 years, 21 months), inspections are too sparse per tract, and the result would be a composite score.
  • Not chosen: requests per resident. Volume mixes how many problems an area has with how often people report them. It appears only as context.

What the results mean

Solid. With all request types, closing times cluster strongly under every check (Moran's I 0.55 to 0.76). The fast cluster in the north shows up every time.

Less solid. The slow cluster moves: in 2025 it was mostly in ZIPs 38114, 38111 and 38104 (Midtown); in 2026, mostly in ZIPs 38116 (Whitehaven) and 38109. Tract ratios in the two years are only moderately related (rank correlation 0.66, 177 tracts). Without solid waste, clustering is weaker (Moran's I 0.34) and only 3 slow and 2 fast cores remain, so the two big clusters are a solid-waste pattern.

How much it matters. The pattern is not chance (z = 17.1), and the gap is large: a median bulk-trash wait of 15 business days in the slow cluster against 1 business day in the fast cluster. What that means for residents depends on the records. If closure dates track pickups, the gap is real. If one area closes requests at pickup and another closes them later in batches, much of the gap is paperwork. The City, or the H4 audit, could settle this.

Methods

Records. Requests are prepared with the 311 pipeline's own code. City-marked duplicates, records from before the October 2023 migration, and requests with no type or an unknown status are removed. Near-duplicates (same type, within 50 m and 7 days; D6) are dropped. Of 42,292 requests opened in the period inside the city, 211 with a bad close date are excluded.

Typical time. For each type, the citywide median business days to close, estimated with Kaplan-Meier so that open requests count at their current age (D9). Types are left out if they have fewer than 20 requests (67 types, 474 requests) or if fewer than half had closed (8 types, 3,690 requests). Most of the latter are weed cutting on vacant lots and houses (3,092 requests, 94% still open, 93% to 96% in each group). Dropping them hides slow service but does not change the map. A request counts only once its type's typical time has passed (647 recent requests do not yet). This depends on the open date, not the outcome.

Tract ratio. Slow ÷ expected. Tracts with fewer than 30 eligible requests are not shown (24 tracts, 189 requests).

Smoothing small tracts. Small tracts give noisy ratios. For the spatial tests only, each ratio is standardized with the empirical-Bayes method of Assunção and Reis (1999), using the variance of a yes/no outcome. Maps and charts show the raw ratio.

Neighbors. Tracts whose boundaries come within 10 m of each other (queen contiguity), weighted equally: 5.8 neighbors on average, none isolated. Tracts on the city edge have fewer neighbors; the six-nearest check gives every tract the same number.

Tests. Global Moran's I with 9,999 random shuffles of the tract values (p = 0.0001; the analytic z of 17.1 agrees). Local Moran's I (Anselin 1995) with 99,999 shuffles per tract, holding the tract's own value fixed; two-sided p-values; Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate of 5% across 181 tracts. Null hypothesis: tract values are unrelated to location. Seed 20260923.

Context. ACS 2020-2024 estimates for the in-city part of each tract (D20), pooled by group with margins of error. Permits and demolitions per 1,000 ACS housing units.

Limitations

  • A closure is a record, not a pickup. The two clusters use different resolution codes, and the codes are undocumented.
  • The slowest services are left out. Most weed-cutting requests on vacant property were still open, so they have no typical time. They are open at similar rates everywhere, so the map is unaffected, but the service is slow everywhere.
  • Only reported problems count. The slow cluster reports more per resident, and requests of the same type can differ between areas.
  • One season. The main period is three months, and the slow cluster moved between 2025 and 2026.
  • Requests are not independent. Requests on one route are often closed together, so Figure 2's intervals are too narrow. The permutation tests do not rely on them.
  • Tract boundaries are one choice. Other boundaries could shift a cluster's edge.
  • Areas, not people. Demographics describe areas, not individuals, and are not causes.
  • Calendar and later edits. Business days before 2026 use a reconstructed holiday calendar (H13), and the City can edit closure dates later.
  • Not a project metric. The 311 specs are drafts (H10) and the manual audit (H3) is not done, so none of this is publishable under the project's rules.

Checks on the 311 records. Missing close dates are the main risk: 60% of closed requests opened in December 2025 and 45% in January 2026 have none, so those months were avoided (June to August 2026: 0.6%). Requests do not pile up on default locations (at most 68 at one point since October 2023). Every in-city request falls in a tract. Open requests are kept and count as slow once their typical time passes. Tracts with no requests are shown as no data, not zero.

Reproduce

From the repository root, with R 4.3 or newer and the project's packages installed:

R CMD INSTALL packages/memequity
Rscript analysis/311-slow-service/run.R                 # reuses the prep cache
Rscript analysis/311-slow-service/run.R --rebuild-prep  # rebuilds it (about 3 minutes)

Inputs (offline, not committed): data/cache/311/raw_2026-09-25.rds, data/cache/permits/raw_2026-09-25.rds, the demolition snapshot ../mem-demo-permits/shelby_permits.csv and the inspection data ../tn-health-inspections/data with its geocode cache. Boundaries and ACS estimates come from geography/.

Outputs: this page, output/figures/*.svg and output/results/*.csv (every tract under every check, plus the group tables). All parameters are in PARAMS at the top of run.R; seed 20260923. The same inputs give identical numbers. The statistics are coded in R/spatial.R, so no spatial package is needed.
